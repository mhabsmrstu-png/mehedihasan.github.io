About

About Me

I am a doctoral researcher in the Doctoral Program in Computer Science at the University of Tsukuba, supported by the MEXT Scholarship. My work centers on recurrence triangles: small triangular motifs in recurrence plots that capture the local, fine-scale structure of a dynamical system.

I use these features for supervised and unsupervised time-series analysis, with a particular focus on human gait: classifying Parkinson's disease patients from small samples of walking data, and clustering gait by age group.

Before coming to Japan, I studied mathematics in Bangladesh, where I worked on reaction–diffusion and predator–prey models. That background in dynamical systems still shapes how I approach data.

Research

Research Interests

Methods for understanding complex temporal behavior, from theory to clinical data.

01

Recurrence Triangles

Triangular motifs in recurrence plots as interpretable features of local recurrence geometry, compared with recurrence microstates.

02

Nonlinear Dynamics

Chaotic and nonlinear systems such as the Rössler and Lorenz models, logistic maps, and reaction–diffusion equations.

03

Time-Series Classification

Small-sample, multi-class, and unsupervised methods for distinguishing temporal patterns robustly under noise.

04

Human Gait Analysis

Gait dynamics for studying Parkinson’s disease classification, motor-control-related patterns, and age-related differences.
